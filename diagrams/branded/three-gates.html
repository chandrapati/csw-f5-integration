Three gates. All three, or no rule.

A policy that misses any gate deploys nothing to the BIG-IP. Enable Enforcement by itself writes nothing.

1

The provider is the F5 virtual server

Policy is directional: consumer reaches provider. A policy that names the VIP as the consumer deploys nothing to the BIG-IP.

2

Enable Enforcement is on

This is a permission, not an action. It needs write credentials on the F5 account. Left off, the orchestrator stays read-only.

3

The workspace is enforced

This is the step that writes security rules to that virtual server over the REST API.

Once those rules are on the VIP, Secure Workload owns them. A manual edit in the F5 UI is drift and is put back. Turning the orchestrator off, or deleting it, clears the CSW rules and leaves that VIP security policy empty. Tell the F5 team before switching it on.

Lab check before production: whether Secure Workload also writes a catch-all deny. That decides if enforcement is additive, or can block traffic nobody wrote a policy for.
